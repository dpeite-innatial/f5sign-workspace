## 1. Why this task exists

Today possession of the emailed link is authentication ([ADR-0028](../adr/ADR-0028-recipient-signing-token-auth.md)): a session opens already authenticated, and `commitSignature` guards only on that status plus consent. A leaked link signs.

[ADR-0049](../adr/ADR-0049-two-gate-signer-authentication.md) answers that with two declared gates, but its load-bearing clause is smaller and comes first: **passing a gate must mint something.** Without it, the access code protects only the interval before the legitimate signer arrives, after which every link holder is inside hands-free.

That clause needs no gate to be built or tested. With the neutral pair — which is every envelope in existence — the gate is open, the credential is minted on the first request, and the ceremony behaves exactly as it does today. What changes is that the link stops being what admits you to the read-model, the bytes and the act.

Splitting it out buys one thing that matters: **the half that breaks the deployed signer coordinates on its own**, in a small window, without dragging the requirement model, the challenge lifecycle and the two-tier lockout behind it.

## 2. What gets built

**The access credential.** A JWT via `lcobucci/jwt` used directly, 15 minutes, algorithm pinned at verification, `typ` and `aud` required with absence a refusal, and the **closed** claim set asserted rather than the presence of wanted keys. Its signing key is separate from `SIGNING_TOKEN_SECRET`, absent from `.env` under repo rule 4 pattern (A), and named in the load-order header. Do not reach for [`SigningTokenCodec`](../../src/F5Sign/Foundation/Http/SigningTokenCodec.php); [ADR-0050](../adr/ADR-0050-session-credential-jwt-and-refresh.md) §7.3 is why.

**The renewal credential.** Opaque and store-backed under [ADR-0045](../adr/ADR-0045-issued-credential-format.md)'s grammar with no exception — a random body, stored as a digest, plaintext returned once. Sixty minutes, renewed on rotation, under a twelve-hour absolute cap counted from the first mint. Read [`DbalApiKeyDirectory`](../../src/F5Sign/IdentityAccess/Infrastructure/Persistence/DbalApiKeyDirectory.php) before writing the lookup: it is the same shape, it already works, and where the two differ the difference is either a bug in one or a decision.

**Rotation and its three mechanics.** A renewal retires the previous credential; a short grace window keeps the retired one usable; presenting a retired credential after the window clears both digests and ends the session. State is columns on the existing `session.signing_session` row — live digest, retired digest, grace deadline — not a new table.

**Two new [`Authn`](../../src/F5Sign/Foundation/Actor/Authn.php) cases with their edge listeners.** `SESSION_CREDENTIAL`, and a distinct case for the renewal route whose verifier is *signature-valid ignoring expiry, plus a live refresh digest*. Each addition forces `requiresDeclaredSubject()` to answer through a `match` with no default arm. The renewal case must not be expressed as "`SESSION_CREDENTIAL` but skip `exp`" — [ADR-0050](../adr/ADR-0050-session-credential-jwt-and-refresh.md) §2.4 is why.

**The route partition.** Routes 6 to 8 of ADR-0049 §2.3 — the recipient read-model, the document bytes and the commit — move off `SIGNING_TOKEN`. Routes 1 and 2 keep it. Every route is enforced at the edge; no route's protection lives inside a handler.

**And the tenant scope moves with them, which is the half a reader will not infer.** [`SigningTokenListener`](../../src/F5Sign/Foundation/Http/SigningTokenListener.php) does not only admit a request: it lifts the token's tenant into [`TenantContext`](../../src/F5Sign/Foundation/Tenancy/TenantContext.php) — so the command bus re-affirms it on writes ([ADR-0010](../adr/ADR-0010-command-bus-middleware-spine.md)) — **and** sets the connection's `app.current_tenant_id` GUC, which is the sole input Row-Level Security reads ([ADR-0017](../adr/ADR-0017-tenant-isolation-rls-persistence-contract.md)). ADR-0049 §2.3 says the RLS-from-token seam is untouched, and that is true of routes 2 to 5, where the listener still runs. On routes 6 to 8 it stops running, and nothing else establishes the scope.

**So the new listener must reproduce all three steps, not just the admission one.** This is the failure worth naming because of how it presents: nothing 500s and nothing 403s. RLS simply has no tenant to filter by, and every policy on every table those three routes touch stops narrowing. A reader who takes "the seam is untouched" at face value builds exactly that. Bar 8 exists to catch it, and it has to be driven cross-tenant rather than asserted on the listener, because a listener test passes while the GUC is unset.

**And opening a session stops being a race, because this task makes it the first click of every signer.** [`StartSessionUseCase`](../../src/F5Sign/Session/Application/UseCase/StartSessionUseCase.php) asked whether the session exists and then created it — two steps with room between them. Two simultaneous opens both find nothing and both insert.

⚑ **What happened next is NOT what this section originally said, and the correction is recorded rather than quietly applied** (measured 2026-08-18 while building this task, by construction rather than by timing). The plan said `signing_session_recipient_uniq` refuses the second insert and, with nothing translating the violation, the signer gets a **500** — inherited from [ADR-0028](../adr/ADR-0028-recipient-signing-token-auth.md)'s deferred-items list, where it had stood unchallenged long enough for [`docs/LOAD-BEARING.md`](../LOAD-BEARING.md) to build a *Never* entry on it as a worked example.

**It could not happen.** [`SigningSessionId::fromScope()`](../../src/F5Sign/Session/Contract/Type/SigningSessionId.php) is a UUIDv5 of exactly the tuple that unique constraint covers, so both opens produce the **same id** and collide on the **primary key** — which was `save()`'s `ON CONFLICT` arbiter, absorbing the collision silently. No violation was raised and no 500 was ever emitted on this path.

What the race actually cost was a **lost update on the session's birth instant**: both openers wrote an identical freshly-started aggregate, and the loser's `created_at` won. ⚠ **And it was bounded**, which is worth stating so nobody re-derives it as worse than it was: the sequential re-open — a signer returning to a session that has already progressed — was guarded by the `find()` preceding the write, so a signed session was never exposed through the use case. The exposure only ever existed for callers reaching `save()` directly.

So the impact was smaller than documented *and* of a different kind, and both halves matter: a defect described as a loud 500 was in fact silent, which is the direction that does not get noticed. This task is still what changes its likelihood — under the neutral pair, opening the session is what mints the credential, so the race moves onto the path every signer takes on their first request, where a double tap or a preloading browser is enough.

The fix is the same either way, and it is to remove the gap rather than handle its consequence: **create-or-do-nothing in a single statement**, the shape [`DbalEnvelopeRepository`](../../src/F5Sign/Envelope/Infrastructure/Persistence/Doctrine/DbalEnvelopeRepository.php) already uses. ⛔ Note that the previously-stated repair — *catch the exception* — was wrong for a reason that also survives the correction: a unique violation aborts the surrounding transaction, so by the time you have caught it there is nothing left to continue. And `ON CONFLICT DO UPDATE` is **not** the fix either; it is what hid the problem. `DO NOTHING` keeps the row that is already there.

**The published spec gains a security scheme, or it lies by omission.** [ADR-0026](../adr/ADR-0026-openapi-documentation.md) publishes exactly one `securitySchemes` entry, `machineApiKey`, and deliberately declines to model the signing token. That abstention was tenable while the token was the only recipient credential and the surface was uniform. It stops being tenable here: three routes now demand a credential a client cannot discover from the document, while [ADR-0049](../adr/ADR-0049-two-gate-signer-authentication.md) §2.3 argues that non-disclosure is unharmed precisely *because* a route's credential requirement is public and lives in OpenAPI — which is not true today for any token-scoped route. So this task either publishes schemes for the recipient credentials or restates the abstention knowing it now contradicts that clause. It publishes them.

**The renewal endpoint**, plus the `#[OA\*]` updates for every route whose credential changes. Those strings are emitted verbatim into the published OpenAPI document the frontend ratifies, so they are contract, not comments.

## 4. Decisions taken here

Four points the first cut of this task left open. All four are settled; the reasoning is kept because three of them look obvious only once stated.

**The grace window is 30 seconds.** It exists so two tabs renewing within moments of each other do not kill the session: the second presents a credential the first has just retired, which outside the window is reuse and ends everything. Thirty seconds covers a slow round trip and clock skew with room to spare, and against a 15-minute JWT it barely widens the interval in which a stolen refresh credential coexists with the legitimate one. Bar 5 is how it is checked.

**The refresh lookup runs inside the scope the expired JWT establishes**, which is [ADR-0050](../adr/ADR-0050-session-credential-jwt-and-refresh.md) §2.4's intended shape and needs no exemption: the signature is still valid though `exp` has passed, so the tenant it carries is trustworthy, the GUC is set from it, and the row is read under row-level security like everything else. The alternative — a separate exempt lookup — would put an unscoped read on a public route, and it is not taken.

⚠ **That choice has a client-side consequence nobody had written down, and it is a contract point rather than a detail.** Renewal requires *both* credentials, so **a client that loses the JWT loses the session** even holding a live refresh. This rules out a pattern SPAs reach for by default: refresh in a cookie, JWT in memory only. Under that arrangement a page reload ends the session. The JWT must survive a reload, and §6 carries it to the signer app as part of the same coordination.

**The absolute cap is a first-mint timestamp column on the session row**, consulted only at renewal. The obvious answer, and obvious for the right reason: it is written once and read on a path that is already loading the row.

**The JWT's claim set is its own type, neither reusing nor extending [`SigningTokenClaims`](../../src/F5Sign/Foundation/Http/SigningTokenClaims.php).** That type carries no expiry, while this one is a closed set with `exp`, `typ` and `aud` and refuses an extra key. One type serving two credentials with different rules is the conflation [ADR-0050](../adr/ADR-0050-session-credential-jwt-and-refresh.md) §7.3 already refuses one level up when it says not to reach for the token's codec.

**Assurance tier: A.** [ADR-0035](../adr/ADR-0035-testing-standard.md) puts *RLS and tenant isolation* in Tier A and this task sits on it, so its tests carry the Tier A bar rather than the default: risk-based exhaustive coverage including negatives, covered-MSI at the Tier A threshold, and the per-requirement traceability group that tier makes mandatory rather than optional. ADR-0035's Tier A set is enumerated rather than argued per pull request, so **the enumeration grows to name this work** — an enumeration that silently omits the newest member of its own category is the failure mode this repo has already met on `SchemaConformanceTest`.

## 5. Acceptance bars

1. **The credential is what admits.** A recipient passes the gate on one request; a second request carrying only the valid link is refused on the read-model, the bytes and the commit. Driven over HTTP. This is the leaked-link bar and it is the one a session flag would have failed while everything else stayed green.
2. **Neutral is unchanged.** A recipient on `(open(), none())` walks the existing ceremony with no observable difference, and their session mints a credential on the first request. This is the regression bar for every envelope authored before this slice.
3. **The two credentials cannot be substituted.** A signing token is refused on a session-credential route and the reverse. The two verifiers hold distinct secrets, so this is structural rather than incidental.
4. **The JWT is refused when it should be.** Foreign algorithm including `none`; missing `typ`; wrong `aud`; an extra claim in a payload asserted as a closed set. Each sabotage-verified — flip the guard, watch it fail for its own reason, restore.
5. **Rotation survives two tabs and refuses a copy.** Two concurrent renewals both succeed. A retired credential renews inside the grace window and ends the session outside it, asserted as two distinct outcomes rather than one. The reuse-kill is a security-shaped predicate that will not fire in normal use, which is exactly the shape that ships unreachable.
6. **Cross-tenant pairing is refused.** An expired JWT from one tenant with a refresh credential from another finds no row.
7. **Opening twice at once opens once.** Two simultaneous opens of the same link yield one session, one credential pair and two successful responses — no 500, no second row. Driven as overlapping requests, because sequentially it passes today and has passed all along.
8. **The tenant scope survives the credential swap.** On each of routes 6 to 8, a request admitted by the session credential reads **only** its own tenant's rows — driven cross-tenant against a real database, with a second tenant's data present and expected to be invisible. Asserting that the listener calls `TenantContext::set()` is not this bar: the GUC is what Row-Level Security actually reads, and a test that never queries across a tenant boundary goes green with it unset. This is the one regression in the task that produces no error of any kind — no 401, no 403, no exception — only rows that should not have been returned.
9. **The new table state is enrolled in `SchemaConformanceTest`.** That test checks exemptions in both directions and will red if the columns land unclassified — which is correct, and the classification must be argued rather than assumed.
10. **`RouteAuthnDeclarationTest` is repaired, not widened.** Its `the_signing_ceremony_prefix_is_exactly_the_token_scoped_route_set` case asserts that the `SIGNING_TOKEN` route set equals the `/api/v1/signing/` prefix set. Moving three routes makes those sets diverge and it reds — correctly. The one-line repair of widening to a union restores green while converting a pinned invariant into one that no longer says which route takes which credential. Split it into per-kind assertions instead. The `assertNotContains('signing.mint_token', ...)` limb is `BL-38` acceptance criterion 3 and survives either way.
11. **The controller unit tests are not evidence here.** `GetSigningSessionControllerTest` and `DownloadSigningDocumentControllerTest` set the signing request attributes directly and bypass the listener, so they pass unchanged whatever the credential is. The verification burden falls on acceptance tests; do not read their green as coverage of this change.

12. **A reload does not end the session.** The client stores both credentials so that a page refresh still has the expired JWT renewal needs — asserted end to end by discarding and restoring the pair the way a reload would, not by inspecting storage. This is the bar for §4's contract point, and the one a signer app built to the usual cookie-plus-memory pattern would fail.

## 6. Cross-repo coordination

The signer app implements the ceremony against the published contract, and this is ratified at OpenAPI rather than unilaterally ([ADR-0028](../adr/ADR-0028-recipient-signing-token-auth.md) §5). Neither side ships alone: the backend breaks the deployed signer at three routes, and the signer has nothing to talk to until the backend lands. Sequence them in one window — **and put [TASK-028](TASK-028-opaque-signing-token.md) in the same window**, because it changes the token's format while this changes which routes accept it, and two cascades to one team in consecutive weeks is worse than one.

What the signer PR carries, stated concretely because the current shape does not stretch to it:

**Both credentials stored the same way, and both surviving a reload.** §4 settles that renewal needs the expired JWT as well as the refresh, so a client holding only the refresh cannot renew and its session is over. The pattern this rules out is the one a SPA reaches for by default — refresh in a cookie, JWT in memory — under which a page refresh silently ends the session. Worth saying to the signer team in these words, because from their side it presents as an intermittent logout rather than as a contract they were never told about.


- **Two credentials in use where there is one slot today.** `useSessionStore` persists a single `token` to `sessionStorage`; twelve methods on `SigningApi.real.ts` take `args: { token }`. The JWT and the refresh both need storage and per-endpoint routing, and the emailed token stays in the URL for routes 1 and 2.
- **The renewal cycle**: a refusal for an expired JWT triggers a renewal and a retry, with **one renewal in flight per client** so concurrent refusals wait on a single call rather than each starting one. This is the primary defence against the rotation race; the server-side grace window is only the backstop for separate tabs.
- **Session-reuse identity.** The middleware keys reuse on `session.token === to.params.token`, comparing against the URL segment. That comparison must keep referring to the emailed token and must not be repointed at whichever credential happens to be current.
- **The credential has nowhere to arrive today.** The client-side `AuthResponse` type declares the gate-submission response with no credential field.
- **Five routes the partition does not classify.** The client already calls `PATCH …/fields`, `POST …/evidence`, `POST …/complete`, `…/decline` and `…/review-ack`, marked pending on the backend. ADR-0049 §2.3's table covers eight routes and none of these. They need a declared kind before they are built, or they will inherit one by accident.

## 7. Open follow-ups

- **Re-opening with the emailed link mints a fresh pair and resets the idle clock — but not the twelve-hour cap.** Established from the code while answering the signer team, and written down because nobody had: `startIfAbsent()` no-ops on an existing session, but `mintCredentialFor()` runs unconditionally after it, so a link holder always leaves with a working pair. `credentialFirstMintedAt ??= $now` never moves. ⚑ **That gives clients a recovery path with no new endpoint** — a renewal refused for idleness is survivable by re-opening — and it is what the signer app is building against. ⚠ **Past the cap the loop degrades rather than stopping**: re-opening still yields a fifteen-minute credential that cannot be renewed, so a naive client retries forever at quarter-hour intervals. ⛔ **Not a hole in the cap**, and the reason matters: today the link *is* authentication ([ADR-0028](../adr/ADR-0028-recipient-signing-token-auth.md)), so the cap bounds **renewal**, never re-authentication — and with the neutral gate pair re-authenticating is free. [TASK-021](TASK-021-two-gate-auth-model-and-access-gate.md) is what makes re-opening cost something, and only then does the cap mean what its own docblock says.

- **The credential belongs to a link, and no server-side check can enforce it** — [`BL-144`](../BACKLOG.md), found by the signer team building against this slice.

- **No refusal `code` distinguishes *renew* from *re-authenticate*** — [`BL-143`](../BACKLOG.md). This slice makes *your credential expired* the **most frequent refusal on the API** (fifteen-minute credentials), and it arrives as the same undifferentiated 401 as a tampered credential, a replayed retired one and a session past its cap. ⚑ **Deliberate, not overlooked**: [ADR-0049](../adr/ADR-0049-two-gate-signer-authentication.md) §2.3 decides the shared 401 *does not distinguish the cases*, so a finer code contradicts a written decision rather than filling a gap — and the counter-argument is on the record too, since a credential already scoped to one recipient's session discloses nothing to its own holder. The signer team was told to branch on *401 on a session route → renew once, retry once*, which is correct and coarse. ⚠ **What would force the decision is a UI distinction, not a backend one**: a client cannot tell "renew" from "send them back through the gate", so it attempts a doomed renewal on every terminal refusal. ~~⛔ A prerequisite either way, found by the TASK-028 session: `ApiExceptionListener`'s `HttpExceptionInterface` arm reads `STATUS_CODES` directly rather than `problemCode()`, so an `HttpException` implementing `ProblemCoded` has its code **silently ignored** today.~~ ✅ **Discharged 2026-08-19**: that arm now resolves `ProblemCoded` before falling back, and the listener's own comment carries the worked example it was costing — a limiter's 429 published as `TOO_MANY_REQUESTS`, a term the signer app maps to its *invalid link* screen. ⚑ Struck rather than deleted, because *"there was a period when this genuinely blocked any new code"* is a correct record of what changed; what must not survive is the present tense, since anyone about to emit a code would believe they inherit a repair that no longer exists. ⚠ **What it discharged is narrower than it reads**, and the narrower fact is the useful one: the fix buys **edge-born** codes. A refusal that is a `DomainException` — which is every existing implementor — always took the branch that honoured `ProblemCoded`. Found 2026-08-21 while TASK-032 published `AUTH_METHOD_NOT_CHOSEN`, which is a `ConflictException` and was therefore never blocked by this at all. **Whatever code lands is additive** — a client branching on the bare 401 keeps working.

- **The five routes the partition does not classify** — [`BL-142`](../BACKLOG.md). [ADR-0049](../adr/ADR-0049-two-gate-signer-authentication.md) §2.3's table covers eight routes; the signer app already calls five more — `PATCH …/fields`, `POST …/evidence`, `POST …/complete`, `…/decline` and `…/review-ack` — and none of them appears in it. **None exists in this backend**, so this is fixing a contract before anything is built rather than reclassifying live routes, which is why it is a follow-up and not a defect. ⚑ **The answer is already known and is recorded in `BL-142` rather than acted on here**: the `f5sign-signer` session confirmed on 2026-08-18 that all five are operations *after* the session opens and none is meaningful on the emailed link alone, so all five take the session credential. The one that would admit debate is `decline`, and only if a direct decline-from-email link is ever wanted — it does not exist today and the signer's middleware opens a session before reaching it. ⛔ **Formalizing them is an amendment to ADR-0049 §2.3's table, not a line in this slice**: this task partitions the routes that exist, and widening a decided table under cover of an implementation task is the silent-ADR-edit repo rule 7 forbids.

## 8. What this plan got wrong, measured while building it

Three claims in the sections above were false, and none was caught by a test. They are recorded here
rather than silently corrected, because in each case the *shape* of the error is more transferable than
the fix.

**1. The double-open never emitted a 500** (§2, corrected in place). The plan inherited that claim from
[ADR-0028](../adr/ADR-0028-recipient-signing-token-auth.md)'s deferred-items list, where it had stood
long enough for [`docs/LOAD-BEARING.md`](../LOAD-BEARING.md) to build a *Never* entry on it as a worked
example. It could not happen: [`SigningSessionId::fromScope()`](../../src/F5Sign/Session/Contract/Type/SigningSessionId.php)
is a UUIDv5 of exactly the tuple `signing_session_recipient_uniq` covers, so both opens collide on the
**primary key** — which was `save()`'s `ON CONFLICT` arbiter, absorbing it. The real cost was a lost
update on the session's birth instant, bounded by the `find()` preceding the write. ⚑ A defect described
as a loud 500 was in fact silent, which is the direction nobody checks.

**2. The idle clock was decided, documented, and never built.** [ADR-0050](../adr/ADR-0050-session-credential-jwt-and-refresh.md)
§2.3 fixes a sixty-minute refresh life and §2.5 states that expiry **is** the session's idle clock. The
first cut of this task shipped only the twelve-hour cap and the thirty-second grace, so there was no idle
timeout at all — a signer could abandon a tab for eleven hours and resume. ⛔ **No test could have caught

**3. Both absolute-cap tests were unreachable as written.** They probed a twelve-hour ceiling on a session
idle for twelve hours — so before the clock existed they passed, and after it existed they measured the
wrong refusal. Rewired onto a session kept alive by rotation, which is the only state in which a cap can
fire, and the state ADR-0050 says it exists for.
